Zest Instructions

Zest is a simple pattern-matching card game in the spirit of SET, with a dynamic similar to war. This HTML version is for one player.

Each card has five fruits arranged in a circle. Each fruit is a strawberry, lemon, or bunch of blueberries.

A pair of cards is sweet if there is a pattern of three fruits appearing on both cards AND no pattern of four fruits appearing on both cards. Otherwise, the pair is tart. To count, patterns must have the same clockwise orientation.

Gameplay

Basic mode:
  1. You have 100 seconds to score as many points as possible.
  2. Correct answer: +1 point.
  3. Incorrect answer: −2 points.
  4. Five correct in a row: +15-second bonus.
  5. Keyboard shortcuts: S (Sweet), T (Tart), N (Next).
Challenge mode:
  1. Every five points, a new variant of the game mechanic is introduced: For the first 5 points, gameplay is exactly as in basic mode. At 5 points, the cards rotate. At 10 points, fruits flicker. At 15 points, an "inverse" variant is introduced, where the player identifies a missing fruit.
  2. After 20 points, the round type is chosen randomly from these four variants.
  3. At 10 points, the player receives a 60 second time bonus.

The Probability of Sweet and Tart Pairs

The two cards drawn are never the same, and cards with all fruits the same type are excluded. Pairs of cards are drawn at random, with each pair equally likely. The probability that a pair is sweet is approximately 50.5%. Thus, the chance of a pair being a hit or a miss is almost the same; this is critical to the game dynamic.

The Mathematics behind Zest

We define the distance between a pair of cards to be the minimum number of fruits that must be changed on one card to make it the same as the other card, up to rotation. Then a pair is sweet exactly when the distance between the cards is two.

This distance is an example of a metric, an abstract mathematical notion of distance. The most well-known metric is the usual straight-line distance between pairs of points in space. Besides this, many other metrics play major roles in mathematics and its applications. Some famous and widely used metrics are:

  1. the taxi-cab metric, used in genetics and image processing
  2. the Wasserstein distance, used in machine learning and image processing
  3. the root mean squared distance, used to analyze the shape of proteins and other biomolecules.
  4. the Levenshtein distance (or edit distance) used in genetics and for spell-checking.
While no mathematical understanding of metrics is needed to play Zest, the game provides informal exposure to the idea of a metric, and can be an accessible starting point for learning more about them.
